Guides

Tracing the design onto canvas

There are two ways to work from a chart pack: paint your own canvas from the true-size page, or stitch straight from the chart. These are the steps printed on the PDF's "How to use this chart" page.

What you need to paint a canvas

  • Canvas: mono or interlock canvas in your mesh, cut 2" larger than the design on every side (the size is on the At a glance page).
  • To paint: acrylic paints, small brushes, a fine waterproof marker made for needlepoint canvas, and a light box or a bright window.

Paint your own canvas

  1. Cut the canvas (see Canvas to cut on the design's At a glance page) and tape the edges.
  2. Print the true-size page for your mesh at 100% and check that the 1-inch square measures 1 inch.
  3. Tape the printout to a light box or a bright window, lay the canvas on top, center it, and trace the main outlines with a fine, waterproof marker made for needlepoint canvas.
  4. Paint with acrylics thinned just enough to flow, matching the swatches in the thread list. Paint the threads and keep the holes clear; let each color dry before painting next to it.
  5. Use the color chart to settle any square the tracing left unclear: count from the nearest 10-line.

Stitch straight from the chart

  1. Fold the canvas in half both ways to find the center, and start there, following the red arrows on the chart.
  2. Work large areas and the background in basketweave, which keeps the canvas square. Use continental for single lines and outlines.
  3. Stitch the motif first and the background last.
  4. Tick off each 10 x 10 block on your printout as you finish it.

Finishing

Leave 2" of bare canvas on every side. Many finishers like one or two extra rows of the background color around a round or shaped piece: ask yours before you start. Block the finished piece if it has pulled out of square.

Can I have a canvas painted from my chart?

Yes. Paint it yourself, or ask a canvas painter to paint one canvas for you to stitch. Please don't sell painted canvases of the design.
